TYPOLOGY LEGEND
MISSING-MIDDLE SHARE BY DISTANCE FROM CORE

A clear proximity gradient: missing-middle share runs highest within a mile or two of downtown and falls off with distance (r = -0.151 between distance-to-core and missing-middle status, n=218,009) — consistent with mid-density housing concentrating near the historic urban core rather than being spread evenly across the county.

TYPOLOGY BY DECADE BUILT

Composition of residential stock built each decade, by typology. Missing-middle share correlates weakly negative with year built (r = -0.069) — a mild tilt toward older housing stock, not a strong era effect on its own.

TYPOLOGY BY ZONING DISTRICT

Raw typology counts within each zoning district — shows where volume sits, not concentration. See "Zoning codes most concentrated with missing-middle" below for the share-within-code view, which tells a sharper story than raw counts alone.

MISSING-MIDDLE SHARE BY LOT SIZE

Not a straight line — a U-shape. Share runs 29.3% on the smallest lots (0-0.05 ac), bottoms out around 1.7% at the typical single-family size (0.15-0.2 ac), then climbs back to 41.0% on 2+ acre parcels (large multi-family/apartment sites). The linear correlation alone (r = +0.197) would miss this shape entirely — both tails drive it, not a steady trend.

MISSING-MIDDLE SHARE BY BUILDING SIZE

Same U-shape as lot size: 10.1% share at 600-900 sqft (casita/small-unit range), down to 2.3% at 1500-1800 sqft (the typical single-family range), back up to 16.7% at 3500+ sqft (large apartment buildings). The linear read (r = +0.068) is weak and misleading on its own — size predicts missing-middle status at both extremes, not in one direction.

ZONING CODES MOST CONCENTRATED WITH MISSING-MIDDLE

Share of parcels within each zoning code that are missing-middle (min. 100 parcels per code) — not raw counts. Uses the City of El Paso's official zoning layer (clean labels), not EPCAD's raw GIS field, which had inconsistent duplicate spellings for the same district.

OWNERSHIP SIGNAL — HOMESTEAD EXEMPTION BY TYPOLOGY

Texas's homestead exemption legally requires owner-occupancy as a primary residence, straight from EPCAD's own current parcel data — the strongest single correlate of missing-middle status found this round (r = -0.27).

SHAPE FEATURES — BUILDING FOOTPRINTS VS. RECORDED STRUCTURES

Microsoft building-footprint polygons spatially joined to parcels, phase one of a footprint-first / imagery-later shape-detection approach (adapting Salazar-Miranda & Talen's CV methodology to available data). Of single-family parcels with footprint coverage, show more dwelling-sized footprints than EPCAD has recorded structures (a candidate undocumented ADU/casita), and show footprint area exceeding recorded square footage by >1.5x (a candidate undocumented addition). Caveat: footprint polygons are noisy (split roofs, oversized garages) — read this as a screening signal to prioritize review, not a confirmed count.

VALIDATION — EPCAD VS. ACS UNITS-IN-STRUCTURE

Each dot is one of 182 census tracts: x-axis is EPCAD's unit-weighted missing-middle share, y-axis is the Census ACS's self-reported share (table B25024, 2020-2024 5-year). Points on the diagonal line mean the two sources agree for that tract. Countywide, EPCAD (20.72%) and ACS (23.05%) are close — but tract-level mean absolute difference is 7.16 points, meaning real local disagreement cancels out in the county average. Adding the structural model's high-confidence candidates as extra units does not close this gap (mean diff moves to 7.28, worse) — the discrepancy isn't primarily "undocumented units the model can find."

Scope caveat: the figures above count any non-single-family structure, including large (31-99 and 100+ unit) apartment buildings — not "missing middle" in the usual 2-9-unit sense. Re-scoped to 2-9 unit structures only, EPCAD (9.15%) undercounts ACS (12.40%) by more, not less — a real ~3-point gap, with model candidates again failing to close it (9.15% → 9.88% vs. ACS's 12.40%; mean tract-level difference moves from 8.77 to 8.87, slightly worse). See data/narrow_mm_comparison_summary.json.

CANDIDATE DETECTOR — WHAT GIVES IT AWAY

A classifier trained only on structural features (never told a parcel's EPCAD code) separates missing-middle from single-family with test AUC. Scored against every single-family-labeled parcel, it flags lower-confidence and at ≥80% confidence as candidates the county's own classification may have missed — shown on the map as hollow markers.
